Which NFC tags for your packs

In short

Get on-metal (anti-metal) NFC tags with an NTAG213 chip, round, 20–25 mm. A regular NFC sticker stuck on a pack often won’t read at all.

Why a regular tag doesn’t work

A LiPo pack has aluminium foil under its wrap, with wires and a plug right next to it. Metal damps the NFC field, so a regular sticker stuck straight on the pack answers weakly or not at all. An on-metal tag has a ferrite layer under its antenna that shields it from the metal.

What to look for

  • On-metal or anti-metal in the name. This matters most.
  • An NTAG213 chip. The app writes a short link on the tag, so its memory is plenty. NTAG215 and NTAG216 work too.
  • 20–25 mm. Smaller is harder to hit with the phone, bigger may not fit a small pack.
  • No MIFARE Classic. iPhones and some Android phones can’t read it.

In the shop’s search, try “NTAG213 anti-metal” or “NFC on-metal sticker”.

Where to stick it

On a flat, wide side of the pack, away from the balance lead and the main plug, where the strap won’t cover it. Hold an iPhone with its top edge to the tag, most Android phones with the middle of their back.

Writing the tag in the app

On the pack’s screen, choose “NFC tag” and hold the tag to the phone. The app writes the pack’s link on it. From then on, holding the phone to the pack opens its screen, even when the app is closed.

No NFC: QR stickers

If your phone has no NFC or you’d rather not buy tags, print QR stickers: Settings → QR stickers. You scan the code with the camera on the Scan tab.
